**Usage note:** This section is intended to help agents understand where Mayday overlaps with competitors and how to position the differences accurately. Do not claim Mayday is universally “better” than a competitor. Position Mayday’s advantages in relation to the customer’s GL, workflows and requirements. Competitor capabilities should be re-checked before use in customer-facing comparative content.

## **Flowrev**

**Website:**  ⁠Flowrev

### **What it is**

Flowrev is a specialist accrual-accounting product for Xero and QuickBooks Online. Its core focus is revenue and cost recognition, including deferred revenue, unbilled AR and prepaid expenses/COGS. It supports different recognition methods and includes reporting for metrics such as MRR, ARR, churn, deferred revenue balances and revenue waterfalls.

### **Where it overlaps with Mayday**

The primary overlap is with:

- Deferred Revenue
- Prepayments
- To a lesser extent, Accruals

Flowrev is therefore primarily a **workflow-specific competitor**, rather than a direct competitor to the complete Mayday suite.

### **Mayday positioning**

Mayday’s advantage is breadth across month end. A finance team can manage deferred revenue and prepayments alongside accruals, close management, balance sheet reconciliations, P&L variance analysis and intercompany workflows within the wider Mayday suite.

Do not position Flowrev as incapable or simplistic. Its specialisation is potentially a strength for customers with complex revenue-recognition requirements: it explicitly supports subscription, SaaS, usage-based, percentage-complete and milestone models, as well as ASC 606 and IFRS 15 compliance.

**Position Mayday as:** the stronger option when the customer wants to improve **month end as a whole**, rather than purchase a dedicated revenue/cost recognition application.

## **RecHound**

**Website:**  ⁠RecHound

### **What it is**

RecHound is a Xero-native balance sheet reconciliation platform. Its current proposition has expanded beyond basic recs: it offers a central reconciliation workspace, transaction clearing, workpapers, retrospective change detection, intercompany matching, prepayments, revenue recognition and direct journal posting. It heavily positions AI throughout these workflows.

### **Where it overlaps with Mayday**

There is now meaningful overlap with:

- Easy Month End / Balance Sheet Reconciliations
- Balancer
- Prepayments
- Deferred Revenue

This makes RecHound a more significant competitor than simply describing it as a “balance sheet rec tool” would suggest.

### **Mayday positioning**

RecHound’s centre of gravity remains **balance sheet reconciliation**, with additional workflows built around that reconciliation experience. Mayday’s proposition is broader: coordination **and** accounting workflow automation across month end.

Mayday additionally has dedicated products/workflows for areas such as:

- Close Management
- Flux / P&L variance analysis
- Accruals
- Recharger
- BRAG
- Mayday HQ

RecHound currently describes itself as built natively for Xero, whereas Mayday also supports QuickBooks Online and Intuit Enterprise Suite across the wider platform.

**Position Mayday as:** a more complete month-end suite for teams that need to go beyond balance sheet reconciliation into the accounting work and coordination surrounding the close.

## **Translucent**

**Website:**  ⁠Translucent

### **What it is**

Translucent is a multi-entity finance platform for Xero and QuickBooks Online. Its current offering includes consolidation, intercompany, treasury, group-wide search, budgets, deferred revenue, prepayments and bookkeeping audit functionality. Its intercompany capabilities include identifying balance discrepancies, adjustments, recharge rules and cross-entity bank reconciliation.

### **Where it overlaps with Mayday**

This is one of Mayday’s broadest direct competitors, particularly for multi-entity customers.

Overlap includes:

- Balancer
- Recharger
- BRAG
- Deferred Revenue
- Prepayments

Translucent also competes for the broader proposition of helping businesses continue scaling on cloud accounting software rather than moving immediately to an ERP.

### **Where Translucent is different**

Translucent has capabilities Mayday should **not claim to replace**, particularly:

- Financial consolidation
- Consolidated reporting
- Treasury / consolidated cash visibility
- Group-wide transaction search
- Budgeting

These are meaningful strengths for customers whose primary problem is group reporting or consolidation.

### **Mayday positioning**

Mayday should compete on being **purpose-built around month end**, rather than trying to match Translucent feature-for-feature as a general multi-entity platform.

Mayday combines dedicated intercompany workflows with Close Management, balance sheet recs, Accruals and Flux. It is also relevant to **complex single-entity finance teams**, whereas Translucent’s public positioning is explicitly centred on multi-entity finance.

**Position Mayday as:** the month-end platform for finance teams that need coordination and automation together, with particularly deep workflows for the accounting work that happens during close.

## **Redwood**

**Website:**  ⁠Redwood Finance Automation

### **What it is**

Redwood is an enterprise finance and Record-to-Report automation platform. Its accrual functionality automates calculations, provisions, reclassifications and GL posting, with rules, controls and audit trails. Its wider R2R offering covers journal automation, close management, intercompany, account reconciliation and other finance processes. Redwood places particular emphasis on SAP and large-scale enterprise automation.

### **Where it overlaps with Mayday**

The most obvious overlap is:

- Accruals

At platform level there is also conceptual overlap around:

- Close Management
- Reconciliations
- Intercompany
- Journal automation

### **Mayday positioning**

This is primarily a **market-segment difference**.

Redwood is designed around enterprise R2R automation and complex ERP environments. Its messaging discusses processes spanning entities, geographies and cost centres, and its SAP proposition focuses on automation across the R2R spectrum.

Mayday is designed for finance teams scaling with cloud GLs such as Xero and QuickBooks Online, without requiring an enterprise finance transformation programme.

Do not claim Mayday has greater enterprise R2R functionality than Redwood.

**Position Mayday as:** purpose-built, accessible month-end automation for modern cloud-accounting finance teams, rather than enterprise-scale R2R orchestration.

## **FinOptimal**

**Website:**  ⁠FinOptimal

### **What it is**

FinOptimal is a suite of accounting automation products built specifically around QuickBooks Online. Its core products include:

- **Accruer:** accruals, prepayments, deferred revenue, fixed assets and payroll accruals.
- **Booker:** journal-entry automation and Google Sheets-to-QBO syncing.
- **Wrangler:** real-time QuickBooks reporting.

FinOptimal also offers managed accounting services and products for accounting firms.

Accruer is particularly relevant: it can calculate and book entries automatically, create supporting schedules, handle reversals, recognise settlements against actual invoices, account for locked periods and recalculate schedules when circumstances change.

### **Where it overlaps with Mayday**

Strong overlap with:

- Accruals
- Deferred Revenue
- Prepayments
- Adjusting journals

There is also broader positioning overlap around removing the manual accounting workflows surrounding month end.

### **Mayday positioning**

FinOptimal is a strong specialist competitor and should not be dismissed as merely an accrual tool.

The clearest differentiator is ecosystem and breadth. FinOptimal’s products are currently built specifically for QuickBooks Online, while Mayday operates across Xero, QuickBooks Online and Intuit Enterprise Suite.

Mayday also brings these accounting workflows together with:

- Close Management and balance sheet recs
- Flux
- Intercompany reconciliation
- Recharges
- Cross-entity bank reconciliation
- Group structure management

**Position Mayday as:** the broader month-end suite, particularly for Xero users, multi-entity organisations, and teams wanting coordination and automation in one platform.

## **Numeric**

**Website:**  ⁠Numeric Close Management

### **What it is**

Numeric is a modern close-management platform built around centralising and controlling the financial close. Its functionality includes close checklists, account reconciliations, supporting documentation, audit trails, task dependencies, journal-entry tracking and AI-driven insights into bottlenecks and issues.

Its reconciliation product pulls transaction data from the ERP, supports materiality thresholds, detects changes to previously reconciled accounts, connects workpapers and can automatically submit qualifying reconciliations.

### **Where it overlaps with Mayday**

The strongest overlap is:

- Easy Month End / Close Management
- Balance Sheet Reconciliations
- Flux / variance analysis

It competes directly for the proposition of making month end faster and more controlled.

### **Mayday positioning**

Numeric’s public proposition centres heavily on the **close-management and reconciliation layer**. Mayday combines this coordination layer with dedicated products that execute accounting workflows themselves.

That includes:

- Accruals
- Prepayments
- Deferred Revenue
- Recharger
- Balancer
- BRAG
- Mayday HQ

This distinction is important to Mayday’s positioning: **coordination + automation**.

Numeric may be particularly strong for larger accounting teams focused on controls, audit readiness and sophisticated close management, so do not claim Mayday simply has “more close functionality”.

**Position Mayday as:** the platform for teams that want to both **run the close and automate the accounting work within it**, particularly while scaling on Xero or QuickBooks Online.

## **Double**

**Website:**  ⁠Double

### **What it is**

Double has evolved into a broad AI-led month-end close platform for both accounting firms and internal finance teams. It positions itself around automating the work of close rather than simply tracking it.

Its current product set includes close management, integrated review tools, AI transaction workflows, bank feeds, accruals, reporting, client/practice management and other accounting workflows. It integrates with QuickBooks Online, Xero, Sage and NetSuite.

Double also markets AI-generated flux analysis and an accruals module covering prepaid expenses, fixed assets, deferred revenue and loan amortisation.

### **Where it overlaps with Mayday**

This is increasingly one of the closest **strategic** competitors because both businesses are positioning around doing more of month end within one platform.

Overlap includes:

- Close Management
- Reconciliations/review
- Accruals
- Prepayments
- Deferred Revenue
- Flux / variance analysis
- Accounting practice workflows

### **Mayday positioning**

Do **not** position Double as merely a bookkeeping or checklist product. Its current positioning explicitly targets internal finance teams and month-end close automation.

Mayday’s clearest areas of differentiation are its depth in **multi-entity month-end accounting** and its dedicated products for:

- Intercompany reconciliation through Balancer
- Intercompany and departmental recharges through Recharger
- Cross-entity Xero bank reconciliation through BRAG
- Group-wide Chart of Accounts, Tracking Categories and Contacts through Mayday HQ

Mayday also has a particularly strong strategic story around scaling finance teams using Xero/QBO and combining **core accounting workflows + close coordination + intercompany**.

**Position Mayday as:** a complete month-end suite with particularly deep multi-entity and intercompany workflows, rather than competing solely on generic AI close automation.

# **Competitive positioning summary**

Mayday should **not** claim that competitors only solve individual workflows. Several now have genuinely broad offerings.

The stronger competitive argument is:

**Mayday brings the work of month end together: core accounting workflows, close coordination and deep intercompany automation, built around the cloud accounting systems scaling finance teams already use.**

The competitive set can broadly be understood as:

| **Competitor** | **Strongest territory** | **Mayday’s strongest differentiation** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flowrev | Revenue/cost recognition | Wider month-end suite |
| RecHound | Xero balance sheet recs | Broader close + accounting automation |
| Translucent | Multi-entity + consolidation | Month-end specialisation; single + multi-entity |
| Redwood | Enterprise R2R automation | Cloud-GL/mid-market fit and accessibility |
| FinOptimal | QBO accounting automation | Multi-GL support + close + intercompany |
| Numeric | Close management + recs | Execution of accounting workflows alongside coordination |
| Double | AI-led month-end close | Deep intercompany and multi-entity month-end workflows |

## **Core rule for agents**

When comparing Mayday with competitors, lead with the customer’s **specific month-end problem**, not a blanket “Mayday does more” claim.

Mayday’s strongest overall competitive position is the combination of:

**Core month-end workflows + Close Management + Intercompany**

This combination is more useful and defensible than claiming superiority in every individual feature.
