# ICP Definition — Mayday

Last updated: 2026-08-18

Mayday automates the manual work of month end — deferred revenue, prepayments,
accruals, intercompany reconciliation, recharges and variance analysis — for
finance teams running Xero, QuickBooks Online or Intuit Enterprise Suite.

---

## Audiences

One ICP, two channels. These are not three equivalent target markets and should
not share an outbound motion.

| Audience | Role | Motion |
|----------|------|--------|
| In-house finance leaders | **The ICP.** Primary users and buyers. Everything below describes this group. | Direct outbound and self-serve |
| Fractional CFOs | Influence tooling decisions across a portfolio of clients | Partner recruitment — separate plan |
| Practice partners | Accounting firms driving adoption via practice licences and referrals | Channel sales — separate plan |

CFO Techstack sits across all three as a nurture and distribution layer, and is
the soft entry point for cold outreach.

---

## Ideal Customer Profile

In-house finance teams at growing businesses that need to close month end,
produce management information and keep control as complexity increases. They
feel the limits of spreadsheets but do not want an ERP.

### Firmographics

| Dimension | Ideal range | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Entity count | 6–20 legal entities | **The strongest predictor of value and ACV we have.** Median across long-retained customers is 6. Single-entity accounts retain but monetise at roughly a third of the base. |
| Finance team size | 2–10 directly employed | Below 2 usually means finance is outsourced. Above 10 starts to signal ERP evaluation. |
| Revenue | >£5m, core band to be confirmed | Floor is a working assumption, not validated against the customer base. See Open Questions. |
| Geography | Australia and UK (primary); New Zealand and South Africa (secondary) | Australia is the largest market by customer count, ahead of the UK. NZ and ZA are 15% of long-retained customers between them. |
| Business model | Subscription, retainer, or contracted multi-period revenue | Generates the deferred revenue and prepayment schedules the product exists to handle. |
| Growth | Total or finance headcount up materially year on year | |
| Funding | Institutional round closed in last 18 months | Budget plus growth pressure. Not required. |
| Ownership structure | Group with a parent and trading subsidiaries; multi-currency a plus | Intercompany and recharge pain scales with this. |

### Technographics

**Strong fit:**
- Runs Xero — roughly 99% of the customer base, and the primary target
- Runs QuickBooks Online or Intuit Enterprise Suite — supported, secondary priority
- Multiple entities on the same ledger, consolidated manually in spreadsheets
- Already running 2+ Xero/QBO ecosystem apps (Dext, ApprovalMax, Fathom, Joiin, Lightyear, Syft) — they habitually buy tools
- Multi-currency across entities

**Weak fit:**
- Single entity, low transaction volume, no deferred revenue or prepayments
- Ledger unconfirmed — treat as unqualified rather than probable
- Spreadsheet-only finance function with no ecosystem apps — process immaturity, longer cycle

**Disqualifying:**
- NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Dynamics 365 F&O or SAP in place
- ERP implementation underway or announced

*Note: 'switching to Xero from an ERP' is a potentially interesting future
segment, but out of scope for current targeting.*

### Organisational signals

- At least 2 directly employed finance staff, excluding external accountants and interim contractors
- Finance has been brought in-house from an external accountant
- Decision makers on staff: CFO, Finance Director, Head of Finance, Financial Controller
- Below roughly £30m revenue the champion and economic buyer are typically the same person
- Above that, the Financial Controller owns the pain and the CFO or FD signs

### Trigger events

| Trigger | Where it surfaces |
|---------|-------------------|
| Finance role advertised naming Xero or QBO | Job boards, careers pages — single best signal, confirms ledger and timing at once |
| New CFO, FD, Head of Finance or Financial Controller appointed | LinkedIn job changes; new leaders re-tool in their first 90 days |
| New entity incorporated, or an acquisition completed | Companies House filings — multi-entity pain arrives on a known date |
| Funding round closed | Funding databases, press |
| Financial year end approaching | Companies House accounting reference date |
| Finance lead departs | LinkedIn — the spreadsheet owner leaving creates urgency |

## Tier Definitions

Tiers are built on entity count, because it correlates 0.86 with ARR and is the
best available proxy for close complexity. Median ARR across long-retained
customers is roughly £1,200, which means outbound economics only work at the
top of this list — the motion assigned to each tier reflects that.

Account volumes are unknown until the first list build.

### Tier 1 — Dream accounts

- 20+ entities
- Multi-currency group
- Xero, confirmed via Tier 1 evidence
- Finance team of 5+
- Australia or UK

Roughly £4k ARR and up. The only tier where human-led, researched outbound pays
back. Named accounts, AE-owned.

### Tier 2 — High fit

- 6–20 entities
- Xero or QBO, confirmed
- Finance team of 2–10
- Deferred revenue or prepayments in the business model
- Australia, UK, New Zealand or South Africa

The core of the base. Signal-triggered sequences with light personalisation,
CFO Techstack as the entry point.

### Tier 3 — Good fit

- 3–5 entities
- Ledger confirmed
- In-house finance function present

Automated sequences, template-based, self-serve conversion path. Do not assign
sales time before a product-qualified signal.

### Tier 4 — Monitor only

Single-entity, or ledger unconfirmed, or below the revenue floor. Interesting
companies that do not yet qualify. Watch for trigger events, particularly a new
entity incorporation or an acquisition.

## Anti-ICP

| Exclusion | Reason |
|-----------|--------|
| Finance fully outsourced to an accountancy practice | No in-house owner of the close; wrong buyer, wrong product |
| Accounting firms and bureaux | Different product, pricing and motion — practice licences, handled as a separate channel |
| On a mid-market ERP, or mid-implementation | The problem is already solved, badly or otherwise; wrong conversation |
| Single entity, low volume, no deferred revenue | Insufficient complexity to justify the product |
| Below £5m revenue | Unlikely to have an in-house finance team |
| Ledger not Xero, QBO or Intuit Enterprise Suite | Hard product constraint. No integration, no deal. |
| Acquired into a larger group | Parent will impose its own finance systems |

## Qualification Framework

### Must-have (deal-breaker if absent)

1. Ledger is Xero, QuickBooks Online or Intuit Enterprise Suite, verified from evidence rather than inferred
2. No mid-market ERP present
3. At least 2 directly employed finance staff
4. Registered or headquartered in Australia, UK, New Zealand or South Africa
5. Above the revenue floor

Unverified is not a pass. An unconfirmed ledger blocks qualification.

### Strong indicators (2+ = high confidence)

1. Six or more legal entities in the group
2. Multi-currency across entities
3. Business model generating deferred revenue or prepayments
4. Finance team of 2–10, recently grown
5. Three or more Xero/QBO ecosystem apps in use
6. Institutional funding closed in the last 18 months
7. Actively hiring a finance role that names the ledger

### Red flags (2+ = deprioritise)

1. Ledger evidence is inferential only — geography and size, no direct confirmation
2. Single entity with no group structure at Companies House
3. No named finance leader findable on LinkedIn
4. ERP terminology appearing in finance job specs
5. Recently acquired, or acquisition rumoured

## Ledger Verification Tiers

The hard gate is the ledger, so evidence quality needs recording alongside the
verdict. Standard website tech-detection tools do not detect accounting
ledgers and should not be used.

**Tier 1 — near-certain**
- Finance job advert from the company naming Xero or QuickBooks
- Review left by the company on the Xero or QuickBooks app store
- Company named in a Xero or Intuit case study

**Tier 2 — strong**
- Named reference customer of a Xero/QBO-only app: Dext, ApprovalMax, Lightyear, Fathom, Syft, Spotlight, Joiin
- Current finance employee listing Xero or QBO in skills or role description
- Current finance staff holding Xero certification or advisor status

**Tier 3 — insufficient alone**
- Geographic or size-based likelihood. Record it, but Tier 3 alone means unverified.

**Negative signals worth scraping deliberately**
- ERP job adverts; appearance in NetSuite or Intacct partner case studies; 'ERP' in a finance job spec
