# Mayday tone of voice

Use these rules for all Mayday copy.

## Voice

Mayday is **clear, sharp, confident, human and finance fluent**.

Write like someone who understands finance, knows the problem and has a better way to solve it.

Get to the point. Use plain English. Be specific. Use the language finance teams use.

Mayday can have energy and wit, but never force personality or manufacture drama.

**Know the problem. Make the point. Keep things moving.**

## Core writing rules

- Lead with the problem, insight or benefit.
- Put benefits before features.
- Use active voice by default.
- Use specific finance problems, not generic marketing claims.
- Write for finance professionals. Do not explain basic finance concepts unnecessarily.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length naturally.
- Group related sentences into complete thoughts.
- Use one-sentence paragraphs only for deliberate emphasis.
- Use contractions where they sound natural.
- Use UK English.
- Read copy aloud. It should sound like a smart person talking to another smart person.
- Cut words that do not add meaning.

**Do:** Your team spends days chasing spreadsheets, checking work and moving numbers between systems.

**Don't:** Manual processes can create significant operational inefficiencies for modern finance teams.

## Do not sound like AI

Remove recognisable AI writing habits.

Do not:

- manufacture drama or artificial suspense
- write sequences of one-line paragraphs
- overuse rhetorical questions
- use repeated sentence fragments for impact
- repeat the same point in different words
- force groups of three
- add unnecessary conclusions that summarise the whole piece
- use predictable contrast formulas such as "It's not X. It's Y."
- overstate ordinary finance problems
- use generic SaaS language

Avoid phrases such as:

- Here's the thing
- The truth is
- Let's be honest
- Let that sink in
- Read that again
- Spoiler alert
- Sound familiar?
- The problem?
- The result?
- The reality?
- The best part?
- And here's where it gets interesting
- This is where Mayday comes in
- But that's only part of the story
- And it doesn't stop there
- We're thrilled to announce

Do not write like this:

> Month end is getting harder.
> 
> 
> The spreadsheets are multiplying.
> 
> The pressure is building.
> 
> But what if there was another way?
> 

Write the complete thought:

> As businesses grow, month end becomes harder to control. More entities, recs and adjustments create more work for finance teams, particularly when the process still relies on spreadsheets.
> 

If you can predict the rhythm of the next sentence, rewrite it.

## Be specific

Describe the real problem and its impact.

**Don't:** Month end can be challenging.

**Do:** As the business grows, more entities, recs, reviews and adjustments make month end harder to control.

**Don't:** Manual processes are inefficient.

**Do:** Your team spends days chasing spreadsheets, checking work and moving numbers between systems.

Specific problems create credible copy. Generic problems create marketing copy.

## Product language

Mayday is a **month-end automation platform for finance teams**.

Mayday helps finance teams run month end with more control and less manual work.

Use finance language naturally, including:

- month end
- close
- close coordination
- reconciliations or recs
- intercompany
- recharges
- accruals
- prepayments
- variance review
- finance capacity
- manual work
- complexity
- scale

Do not stretch product benefits beyond what the product delivers.

Avoid vague words such as **transform, revolutionise, seamless, powerful, innovative, cutting-edge, unlock, supercharge, streamline** and **optimise**. If using one, check whether a more specific description would be stronger.

## Writing about AI

Talk about AI only when it is materially part of the product or story.

Explain what the AI does and why it is useful.

**Do:** Flux uses AI to draft variance commentary from your P&L review.

**Don't:** Harness the power of AI to transform your finance function.

AI is not the benefit. The work it removes, improves or accelerates is the benefit.

## Humour

Mayday can be funny when the humour recognises something the audience knows to be true.

> 99 problems? Month end doesn't need to be one.
> 

Humour must support the message. Do not force jokes, build paragraphs around puns or sacrifice clarity for a clever line.

If the joke needs explaining, cut it.

## Grammar and style

Use correct grammar and UK English.

Use sentence case for headlines and subheadings.

Avoid em dashes. Use a full stop, comma, colon or rewrite the sentence.

Use exclamation marks sparingly.

Use **and** in body copy, not **&**, unless the ampersand is part of a name or space is deliberately limited.

Use **P&L**, not PnL, P & L or P/L.

Use **reconciliations** when introducing the subject or where clarity matters. Use **recs** for a finance audience when the context is clear.

## Month end or month-end

Use **month end** when it is a noun.

> Month end takes five days.
> 

> Run month end in one place.
> 

Use **month-end** when it describes another noun.

> month-end automation
> 

> month-end process
> 

> month-end close
> 

> month-end work
> 

Test it:

**Is month end the thing?** Use **month end**.

**Is it describing the word that follows?** Use **month-end**.

The same rule applies to **year end/year-end**.

Never use **monthend**.

## Active voice

Use active voice by default.

**Do:** Mayday gives finance teams days back.

**Don't:** Days can be saved by finance teams using Mayday.

Ask who or what is doing the action and put them at the front of the sentence.

## Headlines

Make one clear point.

Good Mayday headlines use a purposeful question, genuine contrast, clear benefit or direct statement.

Examples:

> How much does month end cost you?
> 

> Scale the close, not the workload.
> 

> Give your finance team days back.
> 

> Move month end beyond Excel.
> 

Avoid generic SaaS headlines such as:

- Transform the way you work
- Finance, reimagined
- Unlock your potential
- The future starts here

If another SaaS company could use the headline unchanged, make it more specific to Mayday, finance or month end.

## Channel context

**Website:** Sharp and easy to scan. Lead with the problem or benefit.

**Product launches:** Start with what is new and why finance teams should care. Do not open with "We're excited to announce".

**Lifecycle and sales:** Write from the reader's known context. If Mayday has spoken to someone, continue the conversation. Do not write a personal follow-up like a cold marketing email.

**Social:** More personality is allowed, but the copy must make a point. Avoid LinkedIn clichés, fake controversy and dramatic one-liners.

**Long-form:** Start with the strongest idea and develop it properly. Use headings. Build complete paragraphs. Do not manufacture suspense.

## Mayday is not CFO Techstack

Mayday is **sharp, direct, confident and finance fluent**. It has a commercial point of view and is clear about the problems it solves.

CFO Techstack is **curious, useful, informed and human**. It is editorial and community-led.

Do not use the CFO Techstack editorial voice for Mayday marketing copy.

## Final check

Before delivering copy, check:

- Is the point clear on the first read?
- Does it get to the point quickly?
- Is the finance problem specific?
- Are benefits clear?
- Is active voice used?
- Does it sound natural aloud?
- Are sentence and paragraph lengths varied?
- Have AI writing habits been removed?
- Does every paragraph move the point forward?
- Are repeated ideas removed?
- Are claims specific and defensible?
- Could another SaaS company say this?
- Is UK English used?
- Is month end/month-end correct?
- Is the grammar correct?
- Can it be shorter without losing meaning?

**Do not try to make Mayday sound impressive. Make the value of Mayday clear.**
