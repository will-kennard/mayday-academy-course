# Mayday Content Formats

Use this skill to structure Mayday content in the format used on getmayday.com.

Always follow the Mayday Tone of Voice and Style Guide.

## Landing page

**Purpose:** Explain the value quickly and drive a clear action.

**Format:**

1. H1: One clear proposition.
2. Hero copy: One short sentence explaining the value or outcome.
3. Primary and secondary CTA.
4. Proof: Customer quote, result or credibility signal early on the page.
5. H2: Introduce the product, problem or core value.
6. Capability sections: Group related features or workflows into 3–4 clear sections.
7. H3/H4: Specific capability or benefit.
8. Supporting copy: 1–2 short sentences explaining what the user can do and why it matters.
9. Product visuals or demos alongside relevant sections.
10. FAQs: Answer practical product, audience and workflow questions.
11. Final CTA.

**Output rules:**

- Label copy by page element: H1, Hero copy, CTA, H2, H3, Body copy, Proof, FAQ.
- Keep headings short and specific.
- Do not return long prose where page copy is required.
- Group features by workflow or customer job.
- Lead with value before product detail.
- Use customer proof as evidence, not decoration.
- Keep one primary conversion goal throughout.

## Blog post

**Purpose:** Announce, explain or explore a Mayday product, company or finance topic.

**Format:**

1. H1: Clear, specific title.
2. Standfirst: 1–2 sentences explaining what the reader will learn or why the news matters.
3. Date and author.
4. Key takeaways: 3–6 concise bullets.
5. Introduction: Get directly into the subject.
6. H2 sections: Develop the story or explanation in a logical order.
7. H3 sections where a larger section needs breaking down.
8. Evidence: Product detail, customer examples, data or quotes where available.
9. Practical next step, offer or CTA where relevant.

**Output rules:**

- Label H1, Standfirst, Key takeaways, H2 and H3.
- Find the strongest angle before writing.
- Use descriptive headings that tell the reader what the section is about.
- Keep paragraphs natural. Do not create artificial drama with excessive one-sentence paragraphs.
- Do not repeat the introduction in the key takeaways.
- Product launches should explain what has changed, what the product does and why it matters.
- Company news should lead with the news.
- Use quotes to add perspective, not repeat facts.
- Do not invent product details, customer evidence or results.

## Case study

**Purpose:** Show how a real finance team used Mayday and what changed.

The customer is the protagonist.

**Format:**

1. H1: Customer and strongest transformation or result.
2. Date.
3. Customer introduction: Briefly explain the business, finance team and relevant complexity.
4. H2: Before Mayday.
5. Explain the specific manual process, problem or bottleneck.
6. Customer quote supporting the problem.
7. H2: Discovering or adopting Mayday.
8. Explain why Mayday was relevant and how the customer began using it.
9. Customer quote supporting the decision or use case.
10. H2: Life with Mayday.
11. Explain the new workflow and measurable result.
12. Customer quote demonstrating the impact.
13. Add other relevant Mayday products or workflows only where supported by evidence.
14. H2: Looking ahead, where relevant.
15. Explain what the customer is doing next or what the change enables.
16. Customer quote focused on the wider finance impact.
17. Final CTA linked directly to the customer's problem or use case.

**Output rules:**

- Label H1 and every H2.
- Lead with the strongest evidenced transformation.
- Use concrete before-and-after detail.
- Keep the customer at the centre of the story.
- Explain the finance process clearly enough for another finance leader to recognise the problem.
- Use quotes throughout the story, not in a separate quote section.
- Never invent motivations, emotions, timelines, product usage, results or quotes.
- If evidence is missing, flag the gap rather than filling it.
- Avoid generic claims such as “streamlined operations” or “improved efficiency”.
- End with a CTA relevant to the problem solved.

## Final format check

Before returning content:

- Is every page element clearly labelled?
- Does the structure match the content type?
- Is the strongest idea or result first?
- Are headings descriptive?
- Is the copy the right length for its position on the page?
- Is proof used where it has the most impact?
- Are all claims supported?
- Could the content be mapped directly into the Mayday website?

If not, restructure it before returning.
